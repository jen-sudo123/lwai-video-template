When you use AI to research a topic online, it can draw on websites, news articles, reports, and academic papers.

But sometimes, you want to know specifically what the scientific research says about a question.

That's where Consensus comes in.

Consensus is an AI-powered academic research platform. It helps you find, compare, and understand scholarly research, with every answer connected to the papers behind it.

It's useful when you want to investigate a claim, challenge an assumption, or see where the evidence agrees, and where it conflicts.

General-purpose research tools can use academic papers too. The difference is that Consensus puts scholarly research at the center of the workflow.

It searches the research literature, including preprints, and uses AI to synthesize the relevant findings into answers with citations.

Preprints haven't completed peer review yet. If you want to leave them out, use the Exclude Preprints filter.

The value isn't just finding an answer. It's being able to trace that answer back to the research behind it.

Let's see how this works.

I open consensus dot app, sign in, and ask: Does remote work improve employee productivity?

I'll submit it using the default mode, Pro. Pro searches the literature and writes a cited synthesis.

For a more extensive investigation, I can switch on Deep, next to the question field. Deep runs multiple searches and produces a more detailed review. For this example, I'll stay with Pro.

The response gives me an overview of the findings, with citations, and the supporting papers in the References panel on the right.

Now let's go beyond the summary, and open one of the papers in the References panel.

The Evidence tab shows the supporting passages, in the abstract or the full text where available. This lets me check whether the paper actually supports what the answer claims.

Next, I select Snapshot, and if prompted, Generate Snapshot.

The Study Snapshot uses AI to pull out details like the population, sample size, duration, methods, outcomes, and findings.

Some fields may be missing. Here, it extracted four of the seven attributes, so I'd check the original paper for the rest.

Also, a citation doesn't always mean free access. Some papers still require a personal or institutional subscription.

The goal isn't to collect citations. It's to understand how much confidence to place in the findings, and whether they apply to your situation.

The Filter menu lets me narrow results by publication year, study design, and open-access availability, so I find research that fits the question I'm actually asking.

And I can save useful papers into Collections in My Library, to come back to later, or export the references for my own work.

Consensus can also connect with AI platforms like ChatGPT and Claude, where available. That lets me bring cited research straight into the conversation.

For example, I can combine recent business reports with academic studies when exploring how organizations are adapting to AI.

Consensus is most useful when evidence quality and relevance matter. It helps you discover studies, compare findings, and inspect sources.

But its coverage isn't exhaustive, and AI can misread research. So always verify the results.
