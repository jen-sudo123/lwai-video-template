Think about a task you have to re-explain to AI every single week. You describe the audience, walk through the steps, specify the format, and remind AI what to avoid.

You've already learned to use /ˈkoʊdoʊ/ to structure these instructions clearly. And while you can reuse a /ˈkoʊdoʊ/ prompt, a Skill takes this further, by packaging the entire process into a reusable workflow.

A Skill is a set of reusable instructions that teaches AI how to perform a specific task, or follow an established process.

Think of it like a standard operating procedure for AI. It captures the steps, expectations, and rules you'd normally explain to a new colleague.

A Skill can contain more than written instructions. It can also include supporting files, templates, reference materials, and scripts.

Some Skills can even be invoked automatically, when AI recognizes a relevant task. So you don't always have to remember which Skill to use.

You can also combine multiple Skills. For example, one might define your workflow, while another applies your preferred writing style.

Let's take a familiar example. Imagine you prepare a weekly leadership update.

Your instructions might tell AI to act as a Chief of Staff, review scattered notes, identify important developments, highlight decisions needed, and produce a concise update.

With a Skill, you can save those instructions, include your reporting template, and add checks to ensure the result meets your expectations.

Then, each week, you provide the latest information, and AI follows the established process. The same approach works for recurring reports, meeting follow-ups, or reviewing documents against a checklist.

So, how do you create effective instructions for a Skill? You don't need to start from scratch. Begin with the four familiar parts of /ˈkoʊdoʊ/.

Character. What role or expertise should AI bring to the task?

Objective. What should the Skill accomplish?

Do and Don't. What steps should AI follow, and what should it check, avoid, or ask you to confirm?

Output. What should the final result look like?

Then, add two considerations that make those instructions reusable.

Trigger. When should AI use this Skill? What kind of request should activate it?

And Resources. What supporting documents, templates, examples, or reference materials should it use?

Together, these six questions give you the foundation for a useful Skill.

As you develop it, keep a few principles in mind.

First, make the important steps explicit, as if you're briefing a capable new colleague.

Second, separate reusable context from variable inputs. For your leadership update, the structure and process belong in the Skill. This week's notes belong in your new request.

Third, include examples of what good output looks like, especially when tone or format matters.

Finally, test the Skill with realistic inputs. Check whether it follows instructions, flags missing information, and produces consistent results.

This is especially important before sharing Skills with your team. A reusable Skill can scale a good process, but it can also repeat a weak one.

Not every task needs a Skill. But when a process repeats, and consistency matters, it's worth making it reusable.

Start with one workflow you know well. Capture the process, test it, and improve it over time.

The goal is to spend less time explaining the work to AI, and more time getting value from the results.
