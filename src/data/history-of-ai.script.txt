To understand how we got here, let's take a quick look back.

The idea of AI has been around for more than 100 years, but it really started taking shape in the 1950s.

That is when Alan Turing, known for the Turing Test, wrote a now-famous paper asking a deceptively simple question: Can machines think?

That question became the foundation of what we now call Artificial Intelligence, or AI.

AI refers to technology designed to perform tasks that usually require human intelligence, such as recognizing patterns, making decisions, learning from experience, and understanding language.

In the decades after Turing's paper, AI went through several boom and bust cycles.

One major breakthrough came in the 2000s and 2010s with the rise of Predictive AI.

Predictive AI uses historical data to identify patterns and predict future outcomes.

A great example is Amazon. By the time you click "Buy," they may have already predicted demand and optimized the warehouse to ship it fast.

Predictive AI is extremely powerful, but it was usually designed for specific tasks.

It could forecast demand or recommend products, but it could not easily hold a conversation, answer open-ended questions, or create new content.

And for most people, using it meant working through specialized software, not simply telling the system what you wanted in plain language.

Then, in the early 2020s, a new breakthrough emerged: Generative AI.

Generative AI does not just recognize patterns. It creates new content, including text, images, code, and more, based on the data it has been trained on.

Instead of being limited to a narrow prediction task, Generative AI can understand natural language instructions.

It can help people brainstorm ideas, write reports, analyze information, generate visuals, and solve problems through conversation.

The breakthrough moment came when Generative AI became accessible through chat.

And that brings us to the ChatGPT moment.

ChatGPT, launched by OpenAI in November 2022, was not the first Generative AI model.

But it was the first to achieve widespread public adoption and show millions of people what this technology could do.

It reached one million users in five days, and one hundred million in two months.

It moved faster than TikTok, faster than Instagram, and faster than any consumer platform on record at the time.

By May 2026, roughly three years after launch, ChatGPT crossed one billion monthly active users.

Within 18 months of ChatGPT's launch, major technology companies had released competing models.

Google launched Gemini. Anthropic launched Claude. Meta released Llama. Microsoft embedded AI directly into the tools many teams already use every day.

Today, AI is being used across fields ranging from economics and finance to healthcare, education, public policy, research, and software development.

It helps executives analyze information, automate routine tasks, and support better decision-making.

For leaders and workers, the question stopped being, "Is this real?"

It became, "Which one do I use, and how?"
