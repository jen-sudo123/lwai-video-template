Almost every organization is experimenting with AI. But research shows only a small minority report significant business results.

Using AI, and transforming a business with AI, are two very different things.

For successful AI transformation, strategy is crucial. And an AI strategy isn't just about tools and policies. Increasingly, it's about the future of the business.

Companies are deciding how AI will change their competitive position, what products and services they'll offer, and how they'll create value.

Walmart is a good example. As AI agents change how people discover and buy products, Walmart is rethinking the shopping experience. It's developing AI-powered assistants, and preparing for customers' own personal shopping agents.

That's a strategic decision about how Walmart intends to compete in the future.

For your organization, the starting questions are similar. How is AI changing your industry? What new opportunities and threats are emerging?

And ultimately, what kind of organization do you want to become? And what business outcomes are you pursuing?

[pause] Once you've defined your ambition, you need to decide where AI can create the greatest advantage.

Will you develop new products and services? Transform customer experiences? Improve operational efficiency? Or change your business model?

Infosys, for example, distinguishes between AI-first services, which create new growth, and AI-augmented services, which improve what it already offers.

Lloyds Banking Group has identified five AI focus areas: customer interactions, frontline and relationship manager support, operations, employee assistants, and engineering.

A good strategy makes clear where you'll concentrate your resources. And, just as importantly, what you won't prioritize.

[pause] Now we get to the decisions that make your ambition achievable.

How will your operating model change? Where will AI take on work, or support decisions? And where will people stay responsible?

What technology and data will you need? Which capabilities should you build yourself, buy, or develop with partners?

Then there are your people. Which skills must you develop? How will roles evolve? And how will employees take part in transforming their own work?

You also need governance. What are your rules for security, responsible AI, and human oversight?

And finally, who owns the results? And what are you prepared to invest?

Publicis announced a €300 million AI investment plan over three years, covering both people and technology.

Your investment may differ, but the principle is the same. A strategy needs accountable owners, and committed resources.

[pause] Your strategy needs a roadmap, with priorities for the next 90 days, the next year, and beyond.

Define how you'll measure business value: revenue, costs, quality, customer experience, and adoption.

Review the evidence regularly, and decide what to scale, what to change, and what to stop.

And because AI keeps evolving, your strategy needs to evolve with it.

[pause] So, before you call your AI strategy complete, ask whether your leadership team can answer four questions.

Where are we going? [short pause] Where will we focus? [short pause] What will it take? [short pause] And how will we know it's working?

Those answers give you the foundation of an AI strategy, one that can guide real decisions about investment, technology, people, and the future of your business.
