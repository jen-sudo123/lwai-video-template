Great prompts give AI the right context and the right expectations. That's what lets it draw from the right corner of its vast knowledge, and deliver an output that actually fits your needs.

[pause] To get the best results, we use the /ˈkoʊdoʊ/ Prompt Framework. Character. Objective. Do's and Don'ts. And Output.

[pause] Let's start with Character. Here, you tell AI who it should become. You're setting the lens through which it processes everything that follows. A character activates a specific body of knowledge, a tone, a way of making decisions, and a point of view.

[pause] So you might say: act as the world's leading ghostwriter for Fortune 500 CEOs. Or, be a world-class keynote speaker, advising me on how to tell my story. Or, emulate a top-tier business coach, guiding me through a strategic pivot.

[pause] And this is exactly where most people aim too low. They ask AI to be a junior analyst, or a research assistant. But AI doesn't charge by the hour. Whether you ask it to think like a hundred-and-fifty-thousand-dollar advisor, or a twenty-dollar-an-hour intern, the effort on the AI side is the same. The output is worlds apart. So aim high. Always.

[pause] Next, the Objective. This is where you clarify your actual goal. Not just the deliverable, but why you need it. For example: I'm preparing this talk to persuade our board to increase investment. Or: I'm writing this post to shift how our customers see this product.

[pause] The objective sets the direction. Skip it, and ask only for a format, and the model can't align with your deeper intention.

[pause] Then come the Do's and Don'ts. These are your expert guardrails. Based on your experience, what works, what doesn't, and what should be avoided? For example: don't use jargon or buzzwords. Keep the proposed solutions under five thousand dollars. Avoid comparing us to competitors.

[pause] This is what sharpens the output, and makes it usable in your real-world context. Remember, AI knows a lot. But it knows nothing about you, nothing about your business, and nothing about what years of experience have taught you about getting things done.

[pause] And finally, the Output. Be clear about the deliverable itself. Is it a one-page memo, five paragraphs, mid-length sentences, academic tone? An explanation simple enough for a five-year-old? Or a Slack post that's casual, but directive?

[pause] Even better, include examples. Past reports, documents, emails, or templates you've used before. When you show AI what good work looks like, it gets there much faster. Again, not unlike working with people.
