<break time="500ms"/>Claude gives you several levels of AI capability.

<break time="500ms"/>The real skill is knowing when to use each one.

<break time="500ms"/>The models overlap in what they can do, but differ in speed, capability, and how quickly they use your allowance.

<break time="500ms"/>Start with Sonnet.

<break time="500ms"/>Claude Sonnet 5.5 is a practical starting point for everyday professional work: drafting, summarizing, bringing research together, reviewing documents, coding, and working through tasks with several steps.

<break time="500ms"/>For most leadership tasks, I would start here.

<break time="500ms"/>It gives you a strong balance of speed, quality, and cost.

<break time="500ms"/>Then there is Haiku 5.5, designed for fast, efficient work.

<break time="500ms"/>Use it for quick summaries, simple rewrites, sorting information into categories, or processing many similar requests.

<break time="500ms"/>It is useful when the instructions are clear, and you can easily check whether the output meets your needs.

<break time="500ms"/>Then there is Opus.

<break time="500ms"/>Opus 5.5 is built for demanding analysis, coding, and extended work across tools and documents.

<break time="500ms"/>I would try it when the work is harder or messier: comparing strategic options, analyzing financial information, reviewing conflicting documents, or working through a technical problem.

<break time="500ms"/>Think of it as an option to try when Sonnet's first pass is not enough.

<break time="500ms"/>Give it the missing context and explain what needs to improve, rather than simply repeating the same prompt.

<break time="500ms"/>Finally, there is Fable.

<break time="500ms"/>Fable 5.1 is Anthropic's most powerful publicly available model.

<break time="500ms"/>It is built for hard thinking tasks and for AI systems that can plan and carry out many steps over time.

<break time="500ms"/>For example, it can research a topic, create a plan, write documents, improve them based on feedback, and manage tasks across tools with little human help.

<break time="500ms"/>The latest version also cut down on unnecessary safety refusals on everyday technical questions and made long-context work cheaper to run.

<break time="500ms"/>However, as the most capable model, Fable will draw down your usage much faster than the other models, so it is best suited for teams building serious AI systems rather than most daily individual tasks.

<break time="500ms"/>To switch models, open a new or existing chat and click the model name in the right corner of the prompt box.

<break time="500ms"/>That opens the model picker, where you'll see your available options.

<break time="500ms"/>You'll also notice the Effort options in the model selection dropdown.

<break time="500ms"/>This controls how much work Claude puts into a response.

<break time="500ms"/>Lower effort can reduce time and usage while still performing strongly, while the highest settings are best reserved for genuinely difficult tasks.

<break time="500ms"/>Effort does not reliably control the visible answer length, so specify the length and format directly in your prompt.

<break time="500ms"/>Use Haiku when the task is straightforward and speed matters.

<break time="500ms"/>Use Sonnet as a starting point for daily writing, planning, analysis, coding, and document work.

<break time="500ms"/>Use Opus for demanding analysis or when Sonnet's first pass needs a stronger follow-up.

<break time="500ms"/>Use Fable for particularly difficult or long-running work where the additional capability justifies the usage or cost.

<break time="500ms"/>Try the models on a task you know well.

<break time="500ms"/>Compare how closely they follow your instructions, handle the evidence, and produce something you can use.
