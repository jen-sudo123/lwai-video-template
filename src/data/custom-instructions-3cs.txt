Now, in our quest to personalize AI,
we start with custom instructions.
This is because you can shape them directly and they
make every following conversation immediately more valuable.
Custom instructions are useful because they let you
establish recurring contexts and expectations
once rather than rebuilding them inside every single prompt.
Without them, even experienced AI users
can spend part of each conversation
re-explaining things like who the audience is,
how much detail is useful, how decisions should be presented,
or how critically AI should engage with their thinking.
Custom instructions move that recurring information
out of the individual prompts and into a persistent setting
that the AI reads before every conversation.
And that means that your prompts can focus more
on what is unique about each task
while AI starts with a stronger understanding
of how to work with you.
There are three things worth telling AI
and we call them the three Cs framework,
context, communication, and collaboration.
So context is all about what AI should know about you.
So you provide the recurring context
that helps AI give you more relevant responses.
That could include your role, responsibilities,
expertise, and the people that you typically communicate with.
Then there's communication.
So you can define how you prefer information to be presented.
So think about things like tone, length, structure,
the level of detail, and how much reasoning you want to see.
Now be careful about asking for two things at once here.
If you request concise and comprehensive
in the same profile, you'll get neither.
Especially the newer reasoning models
are sticklers for this level of detail.
And then there's collaboration.
This is all about how you want AI
to approach the work itself.
Should it challenge you?
Should it bring up risks?
Should it name trade-offs between options?
Should it ask you questions or tell you
when there isn't enough information to answer well?
Now once you've written all three,
you wanna save them together as a single document
somewhere that you can easily open and update.
An MD file especially will serve you well
when you're working with multiple platforms and agents.
This will then be the reference
that you'll return to every time
you wanna personalize a new AI tool or agent
so you're not rebuilding it from scratch every time.
Don't forget that everything you write
into these custom instructions
runs before every prompt moving forward.
So you wanna avoid providing information
that's either only applicable sometimes
or otherwise would confuse the AI
in context of your prompts.
Now of course we also wanna keep
these custom instructions relevant.
These custom instructions should evolve as you do.
Your context, communication,
and collaboration preferences today
are likely to change within let's say the next six months.
So as your roles or responsibilities change,
you wanna update your context.
If you repeatedly find yourself asking
for a different tone, format, or a level of detail,
you wanna adjust your communication preferences.
And if AI is getting too argumentative
or conversely too pleasing,
you wanna refine your collaboration instructions.
A useful signal here is repetition.
If you keep giving AI the same instruction
across conversations,
you wanna ask yourself whether you want AI
to know or follow this most of the time.
If it's a yes, then consider adding it
to your custom instructions.
Of course, if it's specific to just one task,
then you wanna keep it in the prompt.
Now the more refined and the more up to date
these custom instructions are,
the more that AI truly moves from a general purpose tool
to one based on you and the work that you need to get done.
