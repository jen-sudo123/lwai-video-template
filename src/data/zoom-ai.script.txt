Zoom AI: Before, During, and After the Meeting

Zoom is already part of your day. Its AI can capture what was discussed, flag what's still unresolved, and turn the meeting into a decision document. Let's see how it works.

Before: Set up AI when you schedule

I'm scheduling a launch readiness review for a new customer portal. Before the meeting, I decide what AI can capture and who sees the results.

I sign in at zoom.us and select Schedule a Meeting. I add a title, date and time, then scroll down to Zoom AI.

I tick Automatically start Zoom AI, so nobody has to remember to turn it on. Then there are two settings to check.

First, who can ask questions about the meeting. The default lets people ask only about the discussion from when they joined, so anyone who arrives late can't catch up on what they missed. You can change this to All participants and invitees. If the topic is sensitive, limit it to people in your organization.

Second, who receives the summary. I keep it at Only meeting host. That way I can check and correct the summary before I share it.

Finally, the summary template. The default is a general recap, but there are templates for different kinds of meetings, like one-on-ones, stand-ups and brainstorms. For a launch review, Project update is a good fit, because it focuses on progress and next steps. Then I save the meeting and send the invite from my calendar.

These choices decide what AI can use and who sees it, so follow your organization's policies.

During: What AI can do in the meeting

During the meeting, I want to stay focused on the discussion. That's where AI Companion helps. I open it from the menu bar at the bottom of the meeting window in the Zoom Workplace desktop app.

Now say someone joins late, or steps out for a call. They don't need to interrupt the discussion or wait for a recap. They just ask AI Companion:

What have I missed so far?

In a few seconds, they get the key points: the launch isn't approved, there are two critical defects, marketing needs a date, and training isn't finished. Alex will report on the defects tomorrow, and Sam will confirm the training date. Each point links back to where it was said. That's why I let invitees ask about the whole meeting when I scheduled it.

For example, I can also ask:

What important questions remain unanswered?

Or:

What are the action items, and who owns each one?

AI can still get things wrong. In my test, it gave Sam a deadline nobody set. So before you treat an owner, deadline or decision as confirmed, check the citation.

After: Turn the meeting into a decision

After the meeting, I open Summaries in the web portal. The summary is well structured, but it carried forward two errors: "customer portion" instead of "customer portal," and Sam's invented deadline.

Because only I received it, I can fix it first. I select Open in Docs, correct the wording, mark Sam's deadline as not confirmed, and then share it with the team. That's the version people should rely on.

Now I want more than a recap. In ZoomMate, at ai.zoom.us, I attach the meeting. I check the date so I don't pick a different meeting with the same title. Then I ask:

Create a one-page launch readiness brief for senior leadership. Include current status, critical risks, confirmed decisions, outstanding commitments, and a recommendation. Separate agreed decisions from your recommendations, and flag missing information rather than inventing it.

ZoomMate drafts the brief from my corrected summary. It keeps Sam's deadline unconfirmed and notes that it had no transcript to work from. Each point is tagged back to the summary, so I can check the evidence. The brief opens in Canvas, where I can refine it before I share it.

A practical note: Use Zoom with your own AI assistant

If you already use ChatGPT or Claude, you can connect Zoom to it. Once it's connected, your assistant can pull in your meeting information, so you can work with it alongside the rest of your business context.

On a business or enterprise plan, whether this connector is available depends on how your organization has set it up. If you can't find it, check with your IT or admin team.

The takeaway

So, set it up before the meeting, let it keep track while you run the discussion, and fix the summary before anyone else sees it.

A summary on its own doesn't move the launch forward. What helps is getting to a decision faster, and you still need to check what AI tells you along the way.
